How to Brew Better Coffee at Home

By Mara Ellison · Updated 12 March 2026 · 6 min read

Most of the difference between a forgettable cup and a memorable one comes down to three variables you already control: the freshness of the beans, the size of the grind, and the temperature of the water. Expensive equipment helps at the margins, but it cannot rescue stale coffee or a careless ratio.

1. Buy less, more often

Roasted coffee peaks between four days and three weeks after roasting. Buy a bag you will finish within two weeks, store it in an opaque container away from heat, and ignore the freezer unless you are portioning single doses in sealed bags.

2. Grind right before you brew

Ground coffee loses aroma within minutes. A burr grinder, even an inexpensive hand-cranked one, produces a far more even particle size than a blade grinder, and evenness is what makes extraction predictable.

3. Mind the water

Water just off the boil, around 92–96 °C, extracts cleanly without scorching. If your tap water tastes of chlorine, your coffee will too; a simple carbon filter solves it. Weigh your water rather than measuring by volume — a kitchen scale is the single best upgrade under twenty dollars.

4. Taste, then adjust one thing

Sour and thin means under-extracted: grind finer or brew longer. Bitter and dry means over-extracted: grind coarser or shorten the brew. Change one variable at a time and keep notes; within a week you will have a recipe you can repeat every morning. For the full method, see our pour-over guide.
